# Presenter notes: Survive 12 Weeks

Press **N** on the host screen at any time to see a short note for the current step. Only you see it. This page is the longer version to read once before the show.

---

## Before you start (while people join, about 2 minutes)

Have the setup screen with the QR code on the projector.

> "Phones out, please. Scan the code on the screen. No app, no sign-up. You'll get a funny nickname, which you can change, and a **secret role**: Growth, Operations or Finance. Your role earns you bonus points, so you won't all want the same thing. That's on purpose."

Watch the nicknames pop up. Read a few funny ones out loud. It warms up the room.

## The 60-second intro

> "Today I'm not going to give you slides. You're going to run a business.
>
> You are the management team of **Spice Route Kitchen**, a cloud kitchen. No dining room, just a kitchen and a delivery app called ZipEats. You have **₹5 lakh** in the bank.
>
> Your goal is on the screen: reach this many orders a week by the last week **without running out of cash.**
>
> Every week three things happen. First, you predict something about the week. Second, you vote on four decisions: discount, ads, ingredients and staff. Third, we watch what happens to the money.
>
> Three advisors will give you advice every week. **Every one of them has a bias.** Your job is to decide who to trust.
>
> One more thing: you can't see how anyone else voted until the timer runs out. Your vote is yours."

Press **Space**.

---

## Each week

**Headline (10s).** Read the headline out loud. If it's an event, read the effect line too.
> "Which advisor do you trust this week, and why?"

Ask one person. Don't wait long.

**Prediction (15s).** The question changes every week. Read it out loud and let them answer. Each question tests one idea:

| Question | The idea behind it |
|---|---|
| *Cash check* (week 1 + payout-delay week) | Sales up does not mean cash up. Most people get week 1 wrong. That's the hook. |
| *The money truck* | Money owed to you is not money you can spend. |
| *Where the money goes* | Most people guess ads. Ingredients and ZipEats' cut grow with every order. |
| *Profit vs cash* | You can be profitable and still run out of cash. |
| *Every ₹100* | The room's own discount vote decides the answer. At 30% off they keep under ₹10 of every ₹100. |
| *Can we keep up?* | A demand spike only helps if the kitchen and the stock can handle it. |
| *Order count / In the bin / Danger zone* | Capacity, waste and cash buffers. |

**Voting (timer).**
> "You can't see what others picked until the timer ends. Decide on your own."

Use **+** if the room is still arguing in a good way. Use **P** to pause if you want to tell a story.

**The split.** Read the closest vote out loud. If there's a coin flip:
> "This is what a divided board looks like."

**The reveal: this is the moment.** Let it play. Don't talk over the costs landing. When the cash number moves, point at the chart:
> "Orders went **up**. Cash went **down**. Where did the money go?"

Then point at the truck:
> "It's on the truck. It's ours, but it isn't here yet."

**Results.** Read the answer and the 💡 line under it. **That line is the lesson for the week.** Shout out the top forecaster by nickname.

### Special weeks

- **Payout delay (the cash trap, middle of the game):**
  > "Your sales are fine. ZipEats is just late. Watch what that does to your bank."

  This is where kitchens die. Let the silence land.
- **Boardroom (bridge loan):**
  > "A loan is cash, but it isn't income. What would you use it for? If the answer is 'to cover this week's loss', that's a warning sign."
- **Emergency loan (red flash):**
  > "The bank just saved you, once. Next time there's no rescue."
- **Last week (Investor Demo Day):** remind them of the goal and the order count.

## The ending (3 screens, Space to move through them)

**Screen 1: title + balance sheet.** Read the title and the line in quotes. Then point at callout ① on the balance sheet.
> "Assets on the left, what we owe plus the owner's side on the right. They always match. That's what 'balance sheet' means."

If the kitchen went bankrupt, point at cash ₹0 and the red unpaid bills.

**Screen 2: the chart + what a CFO would say.** Read the two CFO lines slowly.

**Screen 3: same kitchen, different choices.**
> "Same weeks, same events, same luck. Only the choices were different."

Then the room stats: which advisor you followed vs which one was right. That gap is often the best laugh of the session. Finish with the podium.

## The closing (30 seconds)

> "Every one of those ten endings comes down to three things.
>
> **Margins**: how much you keep from each order after discounts and the app's cut.
> **Payout delays**: sales today, cash next week.
> **Fixed costs**: rent and salaries don't care how many orders you had.
>
> Growth is not cash. The businesses that survive are the ones that grow only as fast as their cash allows."

Optional: press the **Replay** button and play a Quick game. The events reshuffle, and the room usually plays very differently the second time.
